# Tutorials

A tutorial is a **lesson**. It serves someone who is learning: the reader
follows steps you chose, ends with something working, and comes away having
acquired a skill. The reader's goal is to learn. The tutorial's job is to make
sure that happens.

## What belongs here

- A complete exercise with a clear start and a clear finish.
- Steps that are concrete and reproducible, leaving nothing to the reader's
  judgement.
- The expected result after each step, so the reader knows they are on track.
- A result that works every time on a clean checkout.

## What does not belong here

- Solving a specific real-world task for an experienced reader. That is a
  [how-to guide](../how-to/README.md).
- Listing options, flags, fields or APIs. That is
  [reference](../reference/README.md).
- Discussing why. That is [explanation](../explanation/README.md). A tutorial
  may include a single sentence of context and a link.

## Writing guidance

- Title it as a lesson: "Getting started with ...", "Your first ...".
- Speak to the reader directly and in the imperative: "Run ...", "You should
  see ...".
- Show expected output after every step that produces some.
- Choose the path for the reader. Do not offer alternatives.
- Run the finished tutorial start to finish on a clean checkout before opening
  the pull request.
